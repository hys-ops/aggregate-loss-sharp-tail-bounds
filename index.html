Sharp tail bounds for aggregate losses when any k positive losses suffice

Given the Borel marginal laws of N nonnegative losses and unrestricted dependence, we derive an exact formula for the largest probability that their sum reaches a threshold d > 0, and construct a coupling that attains it.

What the theorem says

Fix 2 ≤ k ≤ N. Assume that each marginal law has a Borel set of full marginal measure and any choice of positive losses below d from the corresponding sets for k distinct names has sum at least d.

The formula handles singleton losses of at least d separately. It keeps both the names and exact loss amounts for successful groups of 2 through k − 1 names. Once those are allocated, the remaining contribution is exactly the classical count bound: it uses only the sum of the residual masses and their largest k − 1 entries. The final probability is capped at 1. Both the best allocation and a joint law realizing the sharp probability exist.

The count bound itself is not new. The contribution is its exact placement after the smaller-group allocation, together with a joint law that preserves arbitrary Borel marginals. Without the support condition the formula can fail.

An example that distinguishes names from amounts

In two constructed portfolios, the loss probability at each name and the pooled severity frequencies are the same. Exchanging just two severities between two names changes the sharp probability of total loss at least 18 from 4.655% to approximately 5.063%. This is an exact comparison for these examples, not an empirical estimate.
